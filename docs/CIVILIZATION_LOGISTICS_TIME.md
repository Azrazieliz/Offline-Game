# Civilization, Logistics, Projects, Dispatch and World Time

This document records the current authoritative strategic-simulation remainder. It keeps civilization/world evolution deep and causal without turning the game into a worker, household or vehicle micromanagement simulator.

## 1. Civilization development is multidimensional

Civilizations do not have one universal "tech level."

Each civilization develops along its own set of meaningful vectors, which may include:

- mundane engineering / industry;
- magic / thaumaturgy;
- biology / medicine / cultivation;
- computing / cybernetics;
- materials science;
- dimensional / spatial technology;
- energy generation;
- logistics / transport;
- military doctrine;
- social/institutional organization;
- knowledge preservation;
- metaphysical / Authority-related capability;
- other world-specific development families.

The vector set itself is data-driven. A historical-medieval society may have no meaningful cybernetics vector yet, while a magical civilization may possess extremely advanced teleportation and healing but weak industrial mass production.

Cross-vector asymmetry is expected and desirable.

## 1.1 Advancement preserves civilization identity

Civilization advancement is **not convergence toward modern Earth urbanism, industrialism or generic futurism**.

Each civilization develops along the aesthetic, metaphysical, technological, cultural and social line that defines it unless actual history changes that identity.

Examples:

- a wuxia/cultivation civilization becomes more grand, sophisticated, powerful and institutionally mature **as a cultivation civilization** rather than eventually turning into a modern city;
- an eldritch realm deepens its impossible architecture, rituals, entities and metaphysical infrastructure rather than converging on ordinary contemporary infrastructure;
- a magical empire develops larger, more capable and more intricate magical systems, cities, institutions and logistics without needing mundane industrial modernity;
- dragon/elf/undead/divine/cyber/post-apocalyptic and other civilizations preserve and elaborate their own design language and causal technologies.

Development vectors therefore describe **increasing capability inside the civilization's authored genre/identity**, not a universal historical ladder.

Cross-cultural borrowing remains possible, but adoption is selective and transformed by the receiving civilization. A civilization may reject, reinterpret, ritualize, biologize, magicalize or otherwise assimilate foreign capability without losing its own identity.

Only actual world history, conquest, cultural transformation, deliberate hybridization or player/NPC choices may substantially redirect a civilization's development line. The simulation never modernizes a civilization merely because enough time or development has passed.

## 2. Development follows actual civilization capability

A civilization advances because people, institutions, infrastructure, resources, knowledge and world laws make that advancement possible.

There is no hidden global era progression that automatically modernizes everyone.

Development can accelerate through:

- discovery/research;
- contact/trade;
- conquest or migration;
- instruction from advanced characters;
- recovered artifacts/ruins;
- imported technologies;
- magic/cultivation transfer;
- dimensional contact;
- deliberate Ruler Projects;
- other valid causal routes.

## 3. Knowledge transfer requires reproduction prerequisites

Advanced knowledge can drastically accelerate a weaker civilization, but knowledge is not identical to production capability.

Teaching or importing a rifle, spell, reactor, cultivation technique or dimensional device only produces durable civilization-level advancement if the civilization can acquire/develop the needed:

- materials;
- tools;
- energy;
- infrastructure;
- specialist knowledge;
- training;
- compatible biology/metaphysics;
- institutions;
- supply chains;
- world-law compatibility.

A single artifact may still be usable by an individual without the civilization being able to reproduce it.

Reverse engineering and staged capability transfer are valid Projects/processes.

## 4. Civilization decline and asymmetric recovery

Development vectors can regress independently.

Examples:

- advanced magical theory survives while the mana infrastructure collapses;
- schematics survive while industry disappears;
- cybernetics remain common but dimensional travel knowledge is lost;
- a civilization keeps high-end weapons but loses the ability to manufacture replacements.

Knowledge, infrastructure and production capability are therefore separable state.

Recovery can occur from preserved archives, surviving experts, imported help, rediscovery or reconstruction rather than requiring a full reset.

## 5. Population remains aggregate by default

Population is simulated as aggregate strategically meaningful state rather than routine individual agents.

Aggregate population may track only fields/tags that matter to gameplay, such as:

- total population;
- broad species/groups where strategically relevant;
- workforce/available military pool if needed;
- displacement/refugee state;
- severe disease/famine/attrition modifiers;
- broad skill/education availability where needed by Projects;
- other sparse content-specific facts.

Named individuals are promoted to persistent entities when they become mechanically, narratively or strategically important.

Named births, deaths, families and relationships may exist where authored/relevant. There is no universal genealogy, household, job-schedule or daily-life simulator.

## 6. Resources and logistics are physically grounded

Resources are aggregate in storage/state, but their movement and availability obey the physical/metaphysical capabilities that actually exist.

A source may be a mine, farm, factory, magical spring, summoned supply, dimensional extractor, trade route, Domain facility, captured stockpile or another world-valid origin.

Logistics can depend on:

- distance;
- route/path availability;
- terrain/environment;
- carrying/transport capability;
- infrastructure;
- enemy interdiction/blockade;
- dimensional boundaries;
- storage;
- spoilage/decay when relevant;
- time;
- risk;
- escorts;
- Authority/Domain effects;
- teleportation or other actual movement abilities.

The game does not simulate trucks/crates/workers one by one unless a specific scene requires them.

### Teleportation is a capability, not an exception ban

If a character, vehicle, structure, Domain, spell, technology or other owned system genuinely supports teleportation or equivalent transport, logistics may use it.

The system must account for the actual constraints of that capability: range, target rules, capacity, cost, cooldown/recovery, infrastructure, knowledge/coordinates, interference, world-law compatibility and countermeasures.

There is no rule that supplies must travel conventionally merely to preserve difficulty.

## 7. Route and blockade model

Strategic routes are abstracted causal links between sources, destinations and transport capabilities.

A route can be:

- open;
- degraded;
- contested;
- blocked;
- bypassed;
- replaced by another route/capability.

Blockades matter only if they actually deny the relevant movement channels. An army with valid flight, tunneling, teleportation, dimensional transit or another bypass is not magically stopped by a road blockade.

## 8. Generic Project framework

Any meaningful long-running undertaking should use the common Project framework unless its mechanics genuinely require a bespoke subsystem.

Examples include:

- construction;
- repair/reconstruction;
- research;
- training programs;
- fortification;
- infrastructure;
- settlement expansion;
- large rituals;
- Domain/Core restoration;
- dimensional engineering;
- reverse engineering;
- manufacturing programs;
- world-scale preparation.

Projects may have phases/milestones only when that project meaningfully needs them.

A Project can declare:

- objective/result;
- owner;
- physical/reality location;
- requirements;
- consumed/committed resources;
- prerequisite capabilities;
- assigned named specialists if needed;
- estimated duration;
- interruption/failure rules;
- phase graph if required;
- outputs/world-state changes.

Progress remains lazy/event-driven rather than per-worker/per-second simulation.

## 9. Dispatch autonomy with objective fidelity

Dispatches follow the same broad autonomy principle as military command, but mission purpose is binding.

The player supplies:

- primary objective;
- optional secondary objectives;
- explicit constraints;
- risk tolerance;
- return/abort conditions;
- relevant priorities.

Participants may improvise, reroute, retreat, exploit incidental opportunities, split when justified, rescue others, abort, become injured or die according to actual conditions.

However, autonomy does **not** mean abandoning the assigned mission for unrelated activity.

Example: a team dispatched to rescue Character X cannot decide to spend the mission farming Resource Y merely because it is nearby. It may collect an incidental resource only if doing so is compatible with the rescue objective and does not materially violate its orders/constraints.

Orders are therefore evaluated using priority tiers:
1. mandatory mission objective / hard constraints;
2. survival and explicit abort rules;
3. secondary objectives;
4. opportunistic actions that do not compromise higher priorities.

Characters may still disobey because of personality, loyalty, fear, conflicting Authority or another causal reason; such disobedience is recorded rather than treated as normal mission optimization.

## 10. Dispatch outcomes

A Dispatch can produce more than success/failure:

- complete success;
- partial success;
- objective failure;
- abort/retreat;
- delayed completion;
- injury/death/capture;
- unexpected discovery;
- new route/intelligence;
- resource recovery;
- diplomatic/world consequences;
- follow-up event.

Only important causal milestones are stored; there is no minute-by-minute agent simulation.

## 11. World-specific time and calendars

There is no universal Earth calendar.

Each World/Dimension can define:

- day length;
- calendar units;
- year/season structure;
- moons/cycles;
- weather/climate cycles;
- time ratio relative to connected realities;
- special temporal laws.

The starting historical-medieval World should use presentation familiar enough for immediate player comprehension even if its underlying calendar is fictional.

## 12. Cross-world and offline time

World time advances according to the active World's own temporal rules and any established cross-world ratio.

Travel between realities may therefore produce different elapsed time in different locations when the cosmology says so.

Offline catch-up uses real elapsed device time only as an input to determine how much authoritative world time advances; it then resolves the world through the existing multi-resolution simulation and World Director safeguards.

Offline time does not globally force every reality to advance at the same rate.

## 13. Numerical tuning boundary

Exact values for:

- development rates;
- knowledge diffusion;
- project durations;
- transport capacity;
- attrition;
- route risk;
- dispatch success/risk;
- offline compression;
- world time ratios

remain simulation/content tuning and should be validated deterministically rather than guessed into the architecture.
