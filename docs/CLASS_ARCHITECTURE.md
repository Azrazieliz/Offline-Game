# Class Architecture

This document records the current authoritative class-system design. Classes are real system-recognized mastery states rather than restrictive RPG job slots.

## 1. Core model

- A Class is earned from actual learned skills, practice, mastery and demonstrated capability.
- Classes are recognized automatically; the player does not select a class first in order to become capable of its discipline.
- The protagonist begins classless.
- Multiple Classes can coexist simultaneously.
- There is no universal primary/secondary equipped-class slot limit.
- Classes are non-restrictive. A Swordmaster may still use a bow, firearm, magic, tools or another discipline.
- Off-discipline performance is determined by actual proficiency, talent, Factors, anatomy, equipment and skills rather than an arbitrary wrong-class penalty.
- Pugilist is a valid example of a Class that may emerge from sufficiently developed unarmed capability.
- Class labels are not inert tags. Recognition has real mechanical, informational and social consequences because it reflects an actual coherent mastery state.

## 2. Normal, composite and emergent Classes

- Normal Classes identify coherent developed disciplines.
- When multiple disciplines, skills or Factors become one integrated style, the system may recognize a **specific/composite Class** with its own stable identity.
- Parent mastery remains part of the character; recognizing a composite Class does not delete the earlier disciplines.
- If a character's capabilities no longer fit any known Class, the system may recognize a **genuinely new emergent Class** instead of forcing the character into the nearest existing label.
- Emergent Classes receive stable IDs/names once recognized so they can participate in teaching, appraisal, progression, Chronicle/history, AI reasoning and later Grand-seat consideration.
- Cultures may use aliases for a Class, but the engine tracks whether they refer to the same underlying discipline or genuinely different ones.

## 3. Skills cause Classes, not the reverse

- Skills, techniques, experience and mastery produce Class recognition.
- A Class does not automatically inject a package of new skills.
- A character may learn techniques before any corresponding Class is recognized.
- Learned skills remain unless an explicit mechanic removes, seals or damages the underlying capability.
- Class advancement comes mainly from actual use, training, relevant achievements and development of the underlying capability rather than generic class XP.
- Class recognition can still produce real consequences: improved integration/efficiency of related techniques, better learning/teaching pathways, specialized interactions, institutional/social recognition, access to appropriate breakthroughs and other effects justified by the mastery itself.

## 4. Crown tier - apex mastery below Grand

- **Crown Class** is the intermediate high-end recognition tier between ordinary/composite mastery and Grand appointment.
- Crown is not unique: several beings may simultaneously reach Crown-level mastery of the same Class.
- Crown means the bearer has reached the natural apex of what ordinary mastery of that Class can express without holding the unique Grand mandate.
- A Crown bearer is a valid Grand candidate but is not guaranteed appointment. Dimensional relevance, compatibility, character, actual feats and the needs of the defensive order may matter.
- Extremely powerful beings may surpass ordinary Class practitioners by Rank or raw power without being Crown if they have not actually reached that discipline's apex.

## 5. Grand Class - unique metaphysical office

- **Grand Class is not merely another mastery tier.** It is the unique epitome/seat of a Class and a metaphysical defensive office connected to the Dimensions' homeostatic preparation against final/existential threats.
- For each canonical Class there is at most **one active Grand bearer** across the connected dimensional defensive order at a time.
- Multiple Crown-level candidates may exist, but only one can occupy the Grand seat.
- Appointment is performed/recognized through the semi-conscious dimensional defensive mechanism rather than by a mundane guild or arbitrary menu promotion.
- The Grand bearer must already embody extraordinary mastery of the underlying Class; appointment cannot turn an unqualified being into its epitome.
- The Grand mandate may grant real Class-appropriate authority, anti-existential-threat functions, access to dimensional defensive resources/knowledge or other capabilities, but it does not inject unrelated powers.
- A Grand seat can become vacant through death, resignation, loss of compatibility, dimensional revocation, successful succession or another explicit causal mechanism.
- When Grand status is lost, the former bearer retains their actually learned skills, attained mastery, history and any independently acquired capabilities. Grand-mandate-only powers/Authority cease unless separately made intrinsic through a valid mechanic.
- A former Grand normally remains at least Crown-level in the underlying Class unless their actual capability was damaged/regressed.
- An emergent/composite Class may eventually receive a Grand seat if it becomes sufficiently coherent and cosmologically relevant; this is not automatic merely because a new Class was recognized.

## 6. Class evolution, fusion and Factors

- Classes may branch/evolve when the underlying skill set develops into a different coherent specialization.
- Multiple Classes may fuse into a specific/composite Class when the combination becomes a real integrated discipline.
- Factors may alter, mutate or enable unusual Class evolutions when they materially change the body/existence.
- Universal Classes should generally not use species Factors as arbitrary gates.
- If a style fundamentally requires unique anatomy/existence, treat it as a character-/Factor-specific Class or unique specialization instead.
- Character-/Factor-specific Classes can coexist with ordinary Classes and can themselves become composite, Crown or Grand candidates when their actual structure supports it.

## 7. Attained Class vs current expression

- Class history and current usability are separate.
- If a Sword Saint/Grand candidate loses both arms, a required organ or a Factor, the character does not forget the mastery or lose the attained Class historically.
- The affected Class may become partially or fully unusable in its current form until the character restores the missing capability, adapts, or develops a derived version of the discipline.
- Regaining arms, creating prosthetic/energy limbs, changing anatomy, learning telekinetic sword control, or another valid adaptation may restore the original expression or generate a new derived Class.
- A true loss of knowledge/mastery is possible only through a mechanic that actually damages or removes it.

## 8. Knowledge and appraisal

- Exact Classes are knowledge-dependent rather than omniscient labels above strangers.
- Owned characters and the protagonist expose accurate known Class state.
- Unknown beings may only reveal inferred descriptors until observation, appraisal, intelligence, teaching records, reputation or direct demonstration provides stronger evidence.
- Concealment, misinformation and unusual techniques may hide or distort apparent Class information when justified.
- Grand appointment is usually much harder to conceal because it has dimensional significance, but concealment remains possible if an explicit higher-order mechanic can actually mask that significance.

## 9. Gameplay representation

- Classes can be referenced by AI, training, story logic, appraisal, social recognition, authored synergies, teaching, organizations and eligibility checks.
- They do not replace the actual underlying capability checks.
- Raw stats come from actual training, Factors, Rank, equipment and mechanics rather than arbitrary bonuses caused solely by a Class label.
- The UI surfaces the most relevant/highest-developed Classes, Crown states and Grand office rather than an unwieldy equipped-class list.
- No universal class-count cap is required; relevance sorting handles readability.
