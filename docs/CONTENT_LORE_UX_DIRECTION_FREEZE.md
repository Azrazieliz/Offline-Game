## 1. Protagonist baseline

### 1.1 Relative power

"Early/weak protagonist" means weak **relative to the wider cosmology**, not weaker than ordinary inhabitants of the starting World.

At the opening:
- the protagonist is slightly above the ordinary local-person baseline in raw capability;
- he is still unarmed and without autobiographical memory;
- trained combatants, coordinated groups, dangerous wildlife, exceptional local individuals, low-level Rulers and rare monsters can credibly threaten him;
- he is not locally omnipotent and receives no hidden player-level equalization;
- the gap between his opening state and later cosmological scales is enormous.

The starting World's weak fantasy proficiency therefore does not require the protagonist to begin as a helpless civilian.

### 1.2 Ontology and appearance

The protagonist is **not canonically human**.

Player-facing baseline appearance is player-selected. Human-like appearance, if chosen, does not make the protagonist human and does not create a hidden Human Factor.

The game must not force one canonical visual endpoint onto progression. Factors, forms, equipment, transformations, injuries, Domain interactions, personal World Manifestation and other causal systems may alter appearance, but resulting presentation follows the actual development path.

Extremely nonhuman, faceless, hooded, spectral, armored, draconic, biomechanical, monstrous, elegant, deceptively ordinary-looking or other forms are all valid when causally supported.

The exact metaphysical origin/nature of the protagonist remains spoiler-protected story material unless/when the narrative reveals it.

## 2. Starting World identity

### 2.1 Original fantasy civilization

Real human history is not canonical setting material.

Historical cultures may inspire isolated production choices, but the starting World is an original fantasy civilization and must not read as a disguised reconstruction of one real country or period.

The World remains comparatively grounded only relative to later dimensions.

### 2.2 Established fantasy, low proficiency

Fantasy is an established fact of life from the beginning.

The restraint comes from **low local proficiency/power**, not secrecy.

Ordinary inhabitants may know of:
- magic and supernatural techniques;
- unusual species;
- enchanted or abnormal materials;
- monsters;
- rites, relics and other supernatural phenomena.

However, most local practitioners and institutions operate at low power. Truly exceptional effects are uncommon.

There is no single mandatory local "magic system" imposed over all content. Multiple traditions can coexist, including practical spellcraft, inherited techniques, alchemy, religious/ritual practices, monster-derived abilities, weapon arts, healing disciplines and individual supernatural talents. They still resolve through the common causal/rule architecture.

### 2.3 Daily-life fantasy level

Fantasy influences daily life visibly but does not create a magical-industrial utopia.

Low-grade supernatural capability may affect:
- medicine;
- craft;
- preservation;
- agriculture;
- tools;
- minor enchantment;
- specialized materials;
- travel or communication where a real local capability exists.

Availability depends on actual local knowledge, resources, institutions and practitioners.

### 2.4 Politics

The broad civilization may be described as a kingdom, but there is no rigid centralized-government simulation.

The kingdom is a loose civilizational/political umbrella. Practical control can be exercised locally by settlements, nobles, Rulers, communities, military groups, religious institutions, mercantile bodies or other causal authorities.

The opening region is stable and outwardly peaceful. Peace does not imply absence of local coercion, crime, exploitation, political violence, rivalry or isolated conflict.

## 3. Starting-region opening

The previously frozen opening remains:

- seed-generated coherent starting biome/region;
- generated geography becomes canonical for the persistent world;
- protagonist awakens alone in ordinary wilderness;
- no autobiographical memory or origin context;
- functional knowledge remains;
- unarmed start;
- no mandatory ruin, battlefield, dimensional tear or cosmic object at spawn;
- early exploration before combat is valid;
- no conventional forced tutorial;
- first meaningful hostile encounter is avoidable;
- first settlement is found through exploration rather than a forced corridor;
- no scripted companions before normal gacha;
- no foregrounded ancient dimensional/endgame spectacle.

The emotional baseline is:

**austere beauty + solitude + mystery + understated danger -> human/social connection -> agency -> gradual earned power.**

The game must not treat the starting World as disposable tutorial scenery. It must remain deep enough to support long-term play even after dimensional expansion becomes possible.

## 4. Mature tone

The game is strictly mature in scope and is not designed around a sanitization ceiling.

Mature content includes:
- sexuality;
- nudity where appropriate;
- severe violence;
- injury and gore;
- grimness and austerity;
- exploitation, coercive structures and morally ugly behavior where narratively appropriate;
- adult relationships and ordinary adult life;
- political violence and consequences.

The governing tone is **serious rather than edgy**.

Rules:
- mature material is used because the world, character or event calls for it;
- content is not inserted merely to shock;
- not every settlement is depraved;
- not every antagonist is sadistic;
- not every battle maximizes gore;
- not every adult interaction becomes sexual;
- warmth, beauty, humor, affection and ordinary life remain valid;
- the Privacy/SFW Presentation option remains a presentation mask, not the intended canonical tone.

This document does not alter the maturity/lore restrictions already frozen in MATURE_CONTENT.md.

## 5. Early threat ecology

The most common early dangers are:
1. humans and political/social violence;
2. dangerous ordinary animals;
3. comparatively rare true monsters or supernatural threats.

"Monster" is not one universal biological category. Depending on content, a monster may be:
- a natural magical species;
- a transformed/mutated organism;
- a supernatural entity;
- a spirit-like existence;
- an artificial/created being;
- another setting-valid origin.

Rare monsters should feel noteworthy because they are uncommon locally, not because local people believe monsters are myths.

The first preferred serious elite/boss remains an optional territorial creature grounded in the generated region's ecology.

## 6. Early factions

The opening area contains three relevant factions as already frozen.

Their exact names/composition are content-authored or seed-selected from compatible authored templates rather than fixed by the engine.

The three-faction local structure should create meaningful asymmetry rather than three palette-swapped states. A valid seed normally contains:
- at least one locally established authority or territory-holder;
- at least one faction whose interests are materially different from that authority;
- a third party whose goals/alignment prevent the region from becoming a simple two-side war.

At least one early faction may contain a low-level Ruler controlling ordinary Territory without a Domain.

Relations are state-driven. The starting region is not forced into war merely to create gameplay.

Factions should be understandable through actual interests, history, resources, leadership and local conditions rather than hidden reputation scores.

## 7. Territory direction

There is **no canonical first Territory**.

The game must not quietly funnel every player toward a predetermined village, castle, valley or fort.

Valid first-Territory paths include any causally valid route such as:
- development of unclaimed/abandoned land;
- negotiated transfer;
- inheritance or grant when actually justified;
- conquest;
- protection leading to legitimate control;
- political replacement;
- another world-valid route.

The player may remain territoryless for a very long time, potentially through a large fraction of the game.

Main Territory can eventually be anywhere.

First Domain-Core awakening remains a later major progression event.

## 8. Dungeons and special locations

Early dungeons fit the current region and its established fantasy level.

Examples of valid local forms include:
- cave;
- mine;
- crypt;
- fort/ruin;
- underground complex;
- monster lair;
- ritual site;
- hybrid structures.

They are persistent world locations, not tutorial instances.

A dungeon may be discovered long before the protagonist can clear it.

Dungeons do not universally scale to the player. Their danger comes from what is actually there.

Later special locations may become vastly stranger as the cosmology opens.

## 9. Cosmology reveal

The wider cosmology uses a **slow-burn reveal**.

The player understands immediately that the setting is fantasy, but not the true scale/structure of reality.

Early strange events should usually admit plausible local interpretations. Evidence accumulates over time until broader dimensional/metaphysical structures become undeniable.

There is no giant tutorial cosmology reveal at spawn.

Exact answers concerning:
- protagonist origin;
- dimensional preparation;
- deeper gacha/contract metaphysics;
- hidden identities;
- named endgame threats;
- major recontextualizing truths

remain spoiler-protected narrative material.

## 10. Narrative authorship

The assistant/narrative-production role owns detailed story construction under the frozen preference calibration.

Narrative qualities remain:
- long-form mystery;
- recontextualization;
- metaphysical layers;
- dense meaningful information;
- tragedy where earned;
- morally unclean conflicts;
- coherent antagonists;
- protagonist/Ruler point of view;
- authored arcs subordinate to persistent world history.

The narrative must adapt to the one persistent world rather than forcing history back onto a predetermined route.

Consequences include:
- important NPCs can die before planned scenes;
- factions can disappear, split or change;
- Territory may be gained early, late or never;
- gacha may therefore unlock early, late or not at all for a long time;
- story events must have alternate prerequisites/outcomes where player-caused state invalidates the default route;
- no hidden "correct timeline" resets the world.

The Creative Director should normally experience major twists/hidden answers as a player. Do not expose complete spoiler outlines merely to prove they were authored.

## 11. Character-content direction

All pullable Character Identities remain female as already frozen.

Roster production should prioritize strong identity rather than filling archetype slots.

Character diversity can include:
- radically different personalities;
- body types/anatomies/species;
- origins/Worlds;
- moral positions;
- combat distances and tempos;
- resource systems;
- Classes/Factors/Authorities;
- mobility/traversal;
- transformations/forms;
- World Fantasm behavior;
- development-route possibilities.

A character's turn-based and action-combat kits must be two executor expressions of the **same underlying mechanical identity**, not unrelated kits sharing art.

Examples of cross-executor identity preservation include:
- an action-combat mobility specialist becoming timeline/action-advance manipulation in turn combat;
- stance/resource loops remaining the same resource concept in both executors;
- counters/parries becoming reaction/interrupt rules rather than an unrelated turn gimmick;
- weak-point/mark systems remaining recognizable across modes.

No character is required to fit universal DPS/tank/healer templates.

## 12. Character acquisition and early roster

No fixed tutorial summon exists.

No restricted introductory pool exists.

The first pull uses the real banner/system rules once the sovereignty qualification is achieved.

Because the player may remain territoryless, the game must remain viable before gacha unlock.

Pre-gacha gameplay therefore relies on:
- protagonist development;
- world-born allies/NPC cooperation when causally available;
- equipment/skills/Factors;
- ordinary world systems;
- turn/action encounters designed for the roster actually available.

The game must never assume the player obtained a specific first Manifestation.

## 13. Enemy and boss design

Enemies are authored as causal inhabitants/entities rather than difficulty tokens.

Early encounters emphasize:
- armed people;
- dangerous animals;
- rare low-order monsters;
- occasional exceptional local combatants.

Bosses should be mechanically identifiable by what they are, not only larger HP values.

Boss design may use:
- body-part targeting;
- traversal;
- environmental interaction;
- Authority/counter relationships;
- phases;
- summons;
- battlefield changes;
- action/turn hybrid transitions;
- persistent damage/injury/destruction.

Hybrid action/turn transitions are reserved for encounters where the change in executor expresses a real encounter mechanic or scale change.

Later bosses may become cosmologically extreme without forcing every previous enemy family to inflate to match the protagonist.

## 14. Equipment and item-content direction

Starting-World equipment is primarily physically understandable and locally manufacturable, with supernatural materials/enchantments as valuable or specialized exceptions.

Equipment can still be distinctly fantastical; "grounded" does not mean historically accurate.

Item identity should come from:
- construction/material;
- origin;
- maker;
- ownership history;
- affinity;
- proficiency;
- modifications/evolution;
- damage/repair;
- important events.

Higher Worlds may introduce radically different equipment ontologies without replacing the common item/persistence model.

Body/form compatibility remains causal.

## 15. Civilization-content direction

Civilizations are original authored identities and do not converge toward modern Earth.

Starting-World civilization remains low-power but can possess fantasy-specific practices/institutions.

Development must preserve cultural identity unless actual history changes it.

Real history may inspire details but is never the progression template.

The game is not a social/economic life simulator. Only state relevant to meaningful gameplay/history is represented at high fidelity.

## 16. NPC direction

Ordinary NPCs can remain lightweight until relevance justifies promotion.

Generated NPCs should still possess enough coherent identity to support promotion without feeling retroactively invented.

Promotion preserves:
- same entity;
- known history;
- existing relationships;
- prior events;
- location/faction provenance.

An initially irrelevant NPC may later become:
- companion;
- enemy;
- Ruler;
- Overlord;
- commander;
- teacher;
- political figure;
- major story character;
- playable/gacha-eligible through a valid later mechanism where architecture permits.

The game must not reserve narrative importance only for pre-authored obvious "main-character-looking" NPCs.

## 17. UI/UX behavioral direction

This section freezes interaction/behavioral direction only. **Final visual-art styling awaits later Creative Director visual direction.**

### 17.1 Stable grammar, evolving information

Primary navigation and interaction grammar remain stable as the world becomes more complex.

Progression should expand:
- available information;
- context;
- tools;
- filters;
- discovered metaphysical vocabulary;
- world/Domain projections;

without repeatedly redesigning where core actions live.

The UI evolves in meaning and information scope, not by becoming progressively cluttered.

### 17.2 Progressive disclosure

Complex systems use progressive disclosure:
- common actions stay direct;
- deeper analysis exists when requested;
- internal formulas are not dumped into default surfaces;
- exact values appear where player knowledge and usefulness justify them;
- unknown information remains Unknown rather than fabricated.

### 17.3 Knowledge-limited interface

The UI must respect epistemic state.

It may distinguish:
- confirmed facts;
- estimates;
- rumors;
- contradictions;
- outdated information;
- unknowns.

The UI never becomes omniscient merely because the underlying database knows the truth.

### 17.4 Serious mature UX

Adult/mature systems are integrated into ordinary character/world UX rather than isolated behind a novelty presentation layer.

Privacy/SFW controls remain rapidly accessible.

Destructive actions require clear confirmation and visually/interactionally distinct treatment.

### 17.5 Ruler Mode evolution

Ruler Mode can reflect changing protagonist/world/sovereignty state in its available information and contextual projections while retaining the frozen five-destination structure.

Early use can feel incomplete/mysterious because the protagonist genuinely understands less.

Later use can expose richer dimensional, Domain, Intelligence and historical context as those systems become known.

This is not an account-level "UI level-up" mechanic; it follows actual state/knowledge.

### 17.6 World Mode HUD

World Mode remains compact and contextual.

It should not become a permanent MMO overlay.

Critical combat/traversal information appears when relevant; noncritical information recedes.

Enemy information is knowledge-limited.

Touch interaction remains fast and direct.
