## 1. Overall principle

The target is deliberately hybrid.

The game should not look or sound like a direct clone of any one reference. The production task is to identify compatible qualities from several references, synthesize them into an original identity, and reject combinations that create visual/audio incoherence.

The shared anchors are:
- serious mature tone;
- premium anime presentation;
- strong character identity;
- melancholy / danger / beauty coexistence;
- restrained rather than juvenile fantasy language;
- high emotional intensity where earned;
- no requirement that higher power simply become brighter, louder or more ornamented.

## 2. Character / rendering reference stack

Primary visual-reference mixture:
- Honkai Impact 3rd;
- Arknights;
- Wuthering Waves;
with a strong atmospheric/design influence from:
- Punishing: Gray Raven;
- Duet Night Abyss;
- Reverse: 1999;
- Path to Nowhere.

Interpretation:

### 2.1 Rendering
Use a high-end anime/PBR hybrid:
- clean anime facial identity;
- physically convincing materials;
- strong local lighting/material response;
- stylized rather than photoreal skin/hair;
- high-quality cloth, metal, leather, organic, crystalline and abnormal materials;
- readable silhouettes under dark or complex lighting.

### 2.2 Mood
Avoid the default bright-clean "generic anime gacha" look.

The desired image language may include:
- desaturated or controlled palettes;
- selective strong accent colors;
- melancholy;
- urban/gothic/occult or metaphysical undertones where the World/content supports them;
- restrained elegance;
- severe or austere compositions;
- visual sophistication rather than decorative overload.

Reverse: 1999 / Path to Nowhere / PGR influence is primarily valuable for mood, graphic confidence, atmosphere and seriousness, not for copying their exact UI or setting.

### 2.3 Character distinctiveness
Normal playable characters can be visually bold.

The roster should combine the broad visual freedom of FGO with:
- HI3-level character-action readability;
- Duet Night Abyss-style willingness to use dramatic silhouettes and fantasy weapon/body language.

Characters are not forced into one shared costume grammar.

Sexualized, austere, elegant, monstrous, heavily armored, ceremonial, minimal, strange or extravagant designs are all valid where character identity supports them.

Mature presentation does not require universal sexualization.


## 2.4 Selected core rendering synthesis

The first controlled comparison produced a clear Creative Director preference:
- retain a **mix of Candidate A (Somber Editorial Anime)** and **Candidate C (Gothic Metaphysical Fantasy)**;
- reject Candidate D / the third comparison direction as the final rendering target;
- future iterations should move **slightly more anime-stylized and less realistic** than the first comparison batch.

The resulting provisional house direction is **anime-first gothic editorial fantasy**:
- clear stylized anime faces, eyes, hair masses and shadow design;
- less photoreal skin and microtexture;
- strong editorial composition and controlled palettes;
- austere, melancholic, mature atmosphere;
- selective graphic accent colors rather than uniform saturation;
- gothic/metaphysical environmental weight and uncanny spatial language when appropriate;
- PGR/Arknights/Reverse:1999/Path to Nowhere seriousness combined with the stranger, grander environmental tone associated with NieR/Elden Ring/Duet Night Abyss;
- retain enough HI3/WuWa readability that characters remain immediately legible as playable action/turn-combat units.

This is **not yet the final visual-art freeze**. The Creative Director intends to provide additional visual references before the rendering language is finalized.

## 3. Environment direction

Primary environment mood:
- Elden Ring;
- NieR:Automata;
- Honkai Impact 3rd Part 1.

Interpretation:
- monumental but not constantly huge;
- melancholy and beauty can coexist;
- environments may feel lonely, ruined, sacred, severe, intimate or uncanny;
- environmental storytelling matters;
- silence and empty space are allowed;
- fantasy spectacle is used selectively;
- dramatic spaces should feel authored, not like procedural theme-park biomes.

For the starting World:
- comparatively grounded materials and scale;
- established fantasy remains visible;
- austere beauty;
- strong weather/time-of-day identity;
- serious physical world;
- no requirement for historical realism;
- low local power does not mean visually mundane.

HI3 Part 1 is a tonal/cinematic reference particularly for emotional setpieces, strange spaces and escalation, not for copying its specific locations.

## 4. Violence / mature visual treatment

Exact gore intensity is delegated to context.

Default rule:
- no arbitrary violence ceiling;
- no forced restraint where severe injury/dismemberment is mechanically and anatomically valid;
- no constant gore inflation merely to appear mature;
- serious consequences;
- body/material-specific injury presentation;
- quiet aftermath can be as important as impact spectacle.

The target is mature seriousness rather than exploitation-shock aesthetics.

## 5. UI / graphic-language direction

Final UI art styling remains subject to later visual selection, but broad reference implications are:

- premium character-first presentation;
- strong editorial composition;
- selective use of asymmetry;
- controlled typography;
- layered information hierarchy;
- restrained luminous/metaphysical accents;
- willingness to use darker or muted surfaces;
- graphic confidence similar in spirit to Arknights / Reverse: 1999 / Path to Nowhere rather than generic fantasy parchment or generic sci-fi HUD;
- structural clarity remains more important than decorative novelty.

Ruler Mode visual language may evolve with:
- protagonist state;
- sovereignty;
- Domain;
- World;
- discovered metaphysics;
while core navigation remains stable.

No final UI skin is frozen before visual concept comparison.

## 6. Future concept-art comparison rule

When visual concepts are generated later, do not produce arbitrary one-off mood images.

Create several deliberately differentiated but production-valid directions using the same controlled subject/scene so comparison is meaningful.

Each candidate should preserve:
- same protagonist/character identity where applicable;
- same composition purpose;
- same environment brief;
- same camera class;
- same maturity/tone;
while varying:
- rendering balance;
- graphic stylization;
- material treatment;
- palette strategy;
- atmosphere;
- UI/ornament density where relevant.

The goal is to choose a repeatable production language, not the prettiest isolated image.

Do not generate these images until explicitly requested.

## 8. Music production direction derived from references

### 8.1 Vocals and song language
Vocals are important but should remain event-significant.

Japanese is the **default / most common vocal language**, matching the game's primary authored spoken-language identity.

Song language is not globally restricted. English, German and other languages are explicitly valid when the track, character, World, faction, cultural context or dramatic intent benefits from them. Invented/ritual languages and non-lexical choral writing are also valid where appropriate.

Language choice is therefore content-authored rather than a fixed soundtrack rule. Japanese should remain the dominant overall presence without preventing individual tracks from using another language.

Use full vocal tracks particularly for:
- major story climaxes;
- character-defining moments;
- irreversible losses/breakthroughs;
- selected boss/endgame sequences;
- opening/ending-type sequences where justified.

Routine exploration/combat should not constantly use lyrical vocals, preserving their impact.

### 8.2 Melodic identity
Strong memorable melody is preferred over purely textural scoring.

Themes should support:
- leitmotif transformation;
- tragic reinterpretation;
- corrupted/altered versions;
- instrumental -> vocal escalation;
- sparse -> full arrangement escalation.

### 8.3 Emotional range
Core allowed range:
- austere;
- melancholic;
- mysterious;
- intimate;
- ominous;
- tragic;
- violent;
- defiant;
- transcendent;
- beautiful.

Avoid forcing "epic" as the default high-stakes emotion.

### 8.4 Hybridization
The soundtrack may combine:
- orchestral;
- chamber;
- choral;
- rock;
- electronic;
- industrial;
- ambient;
- acoustic/folk-derived;
- unusual World-specific instrumentation.

Combination is content-driven rather than a global requirement.

## 9. Combat / cinematic sound direction

Broad sound-design target:
- **moment-to-moment combat leans toward PGR / Wuthering Waves-style tactile aggression and physical impact**;
- **major cinematic/character moments lean toward HI3-style audiovisual synchronization and controlled dramatic timing**;
- strong character-specific signatures;
- clear parry/dodge/impact readability;
- material/anatomy/environment-specific weight;
- no universal overcompressed loudness wall.

Action combat should feel immediate, sharp and tactile. Weapon contacts, parries, dodges, heavy impacts and movement should have enough physical weight to make direct control satisfying without turning every action into maximum-volume noise.

Turn combat should preserve equally strong impact identity through timing, animation, camera, hit-stop where appropriate, sound layering and character-specific signatures despite its timeline/menu execution.

Ultimates, World Fantasms, Domains, major boss transitions and story combat may deliberately tighten audiovisual synchronization toward the HI3 cinematic reference: animation, SFX, music, vocals and silence can be composed as one event.

High-order power may become quieter or cleaner when metaphysical precision is more appropriate than brute force.

Silence is an authored tool.

## 10. Environmental audio

Primary ambience direction:
- NieR-like emotional restraint;
- hybrid density.

World audio can range from sparse to richly physical depending on location.

Guidelines:
- wilderness may use wind, vegetation, distant fauna, water and sparse local events without constant noise;
- settlements can become denser and socially layered;
- interiors use strong material/space response;
- unusual Worlds can radically change acoustic logic;
- music and ambience should leave each other room;
- environmental density is not globally maximal.

## 11. Combat format identity

Authoritative production target:
- approximately 75% turn-based combat content;
- approximately 25% World Mode action combat content.

Overall inspiration is a HSR / HI3 hybrid weighted toward the HSR-like strategic/turn side while preserving HI3-like high-quality action combat as a major pillar.

This proportion does not imply:
- exactly three turn fights for every action fight;
- converting physical World Mode encounters arbitrarily into turn combat;
- 75% of total game time being turn combat.

It governs long-run authored combat-content emphasis.
